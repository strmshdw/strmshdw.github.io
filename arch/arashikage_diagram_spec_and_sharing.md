## 2. Deep-Dive Technology Reference (Function, Science, Research Origins & Dataflow Placement)

### 2.1 `5,376-Byte` `C2v` Pitch-Canonical Observation Struct & 6-Octave Fourier Encoding
- **1. How It Works**: C++ writes all 64 arena entities (`6` cars, `1` current ball, `8` future ball trajectory keyframes, `1` match state, `34` boost pads, `6` goalposts, `4` suspension wheels, `4` arena surfaces) into a 64-byte-aligned `5,376-byte` struct (`Hex63EntityNodeArenaSlice`). All 3D arena vectors are rotated $180^\circ$ around the Z-axis when playing on Orange ($M_{\text{C2v}} = \operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, +1) \in SO(3)$). On the GPU, 3D positions are expanded into 6 octaves of sine and cosine waves ($\lambda = 8192\text{ uu} \to 256\text{ uu}$).
- **2. Why It Is Used**: Standard MLPs suffer from *spectral bias*—they struggle to learn high-frequency spatial functions from smooth normalized floats in $[-1, +1]$ (where a `15 uu` dribble adjustment is a `0.003` float change). Furthermore, without team rotation, Orange and Blue observations have inverted signs on all world coordinates.
- **3. What It Contributes**: Rotating into the `C2v` canonical frame yields **4× sample efficiency** across team and lateral symmetries while preserving left-handed cross-product chirality ($\det(M_{\text{C2v}}) = +1$). Sending only the 64 intrinsic nodes (`5.25 KB/step`) instead of precomputed pairwise edges (`98.3 KB/step`) reduces PCIe bandwidth and rollout buffer VRAM by **19.8×**.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Multi-scale trigonometric positional encoding was popularized by *Mildenhall et al. (NeRF, 2020)* and *Tancik et al. (Fourier Features Let Networks Learn High Frequency Functions in Low Dimensional Domains, NeurIPS 2020)*, which proved via Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory that mapping coordinates through $\gamma(\mathbf{x}) = [\sin(2^k\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^k\pi\mathbf{x})]$ turns a stationary shift-invariant kernel into one with tunable bandwidth. Group-equivariant reinforcement learning (`C2v` Klein 4-group) follows geometric deep learning principles (*Bronstein et al., 2021*).
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Sits at the **C++20 $\to$ GPU boundary (Stage 1)** so that every downstream edge calculation, attention head, memory write, and value prediction operates in a single team-invariant coordinate system.

### 2.2 Bipartite `16 × 64` Entity Graph & On-the-Fly `72D` Pairwise Physics Kernel
- **1. How It Works**: Instead of all-to-all $64 \times 64 = 4,096$ attention, the graph partitions tokens into **16 Dynamic Queries** ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{dyn}}$: cars, ball, ball rollout, match anchor) and **64 Keys/Values** ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{dyn}} \cup \mathcal{V}_{\text{env}}$). A fused GPU kernel computes `[B, 16, 64, 72]` directed pairwise features (`15D` relative kinematics + `9D` pair-type collision laws + `48D` 3D/radial Fourier features) in `0.09 ms`.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: `34` of the `64` entities are static boost pads. Computing attention from Boost Pad #4 to Boost Pad #19 wastes 75% of attention FLOPs and HBM bandwidth. In addition, standard dot-product attention ($Q K^\top$) only measures embedding similarity—it does not explicitly encode Newton/Bullet3 collision equations.
- **3. What It Contributes**: Cuts attention complexity by **4×** (`1,024` pairs vs. `4,096`) while injecting exact domain physics directly into the attention logits: the Psyonix `Ball::_OnHit` impulse direction (`z_scale = 0.35`), boost pad arrival time minus remaining cooldown, car-to-car OBB bump margins, and goal opening angles.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Builds on *Set Transformers / Perceiver IO (Lee et al., 2019; Jaegle et al., 2021)*, which use asymmetric cross-attention from a compact latent set to a larger context set, combined with relational graph biases from *Graphormer (Ying et al., 2021)* and *AlphaFold 2 Evoformer pair representations (Jumper et al., 2021)*.
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Sits at **Stage 2 (Encoder Entry)** immediately after stem projection, compressing the 64 heterogeneous arena objects into 16 context-aware dynamic tokens $\mathbf{Z}^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 16 \times 384}$ before recurrent memory or multi-step reasoning runs.

### 2.3 Differential Attention (`DiffAttn`)
- **1. How It Works**: Each attention head splits its queries and keys into two sub-groups ($Q_1, K_1$ and $Q_2, K_2$), adds two distinct pairwise edge bias maps ($\mathbf{B}_1, \mathbf{B}_2$), computes two separate softmax distributions ($A_1, A_2$), and subtracts them: $\mathbf{O}_h = (1 - \lambda_{\text{init}})\,\text{GroupRMSNorm}((A_{1,h} - \lambda_k A_{2,h})\mathbf{V}_h)$.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: Because standard $\operatorname{softmax}(z_j) = e^{z_j} / \sum_i e^{z_i}$ is strictly positive, small background logits across 34 boost pads and 14 structural tokens accumulate into a large fraction of the total attention weight, diluting focus on the ball and immediate defenders.
- **3. What It Contributes**: Drives attention weights on irrelevant background entities to zero (or slightly negative before normalization), producing sparse, high-contrast attention maps that track the ball, active bounce point, and nearest challenger without distraction.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Introduced in *Differential Transformer (Ye et al., Microsoft Research, Oct 2024)*, inspired by differential amplifiers in electrical engineering and noise-canceling headphones, where subtracting two balanced signals cancels common-mode noise. Arashikage (`Hex63`) extends `DiffAttn` by projecting the `72D` pairwise physics edges into two independent bias maps $\mathbf{B}_1 \ne \mathbf{B}_2$ so spatial biases do not cancel out during subtraction.
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Used as the core attention operator inside both the **Stage 2 Bipartite Encoder** and the **Stage 4 `Coconut` Recurrent Cross-Attention Loop**, where suppressing background boost-pad clutter is critical.

### 2.4 `Titans` / `TTT-MLP` Analytical Test-Time Neural Memory (`2×4×32×32` `FP32`)
- **1. How It Works**: Each car maintains a 2-layer neural network per memory head ($\mathbf{W}_t^{(1)}, \mathbf{W}_t^{(2)} \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 32 \times 32}$) stored in `FP32` as its recurrent state (`64 KB/car`). On every step, the previous step's cached key $\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{t-1}$ is passed through $\mathbf{W}_{t-1}^{(1,2)}$ to predict the current step's realized acceleration and impulse vector $\mathbf{v}_t$. The prediction error $\mathbf{e}_t = \tanh(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{t-1}^{(2)}\operatorname{SiLU}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{t-1}^{(1)}\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{t-1}) - \mathbf{v}_t)$ produces a closed-form analytical gradient $\mathbf{G}_t^{(1,2)}$ that updates $\mathbf{W}_t^{(1,2)}$ in place with momentum $\mathbf{M}_t^{(1,2)}$, prior decay toward $\mathbf{W}_{\text{init}}^{(1,2)}$, and Frobenius ball retraction $\|\mathbf{W}_{t,h}^{(l)}\|_F \le 4.0$.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: A single physics frame is Markovian for rigid-body kinematics, but partially observable for opponent intent, boost starvation habits, and multi-second adaptation. Stacking past frames increases input bandwidth and blurs instantaneous `120 Hz` impulses, while standard linear RNNs (Mamba/Linear Attention) have limited expressive capacity because their state update is linear.
- **3. What It Contributes**: Gives the agent a nonlinear 2-layer MLP working memory that adapts *at test time* to unexpected physics events (fakes, bumps, boost steals) while remaining numerically bounded over 4,500-step matches via `FP32` storage and Frobenius norm retraction.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Combines *Learning to (Learn at Test Time): RNNs with Expressive Hidden States (TTT-Linear / TTT-MLP, Sun et al., Stanford/UC Berkeley, 2024)* and *Titans: Learning to Memorize at Test Time (Behrouz et al., Google Research, Dec 2024)*, which formulate recurrent state updates as online gradient descent on an associative memory surprise loss with momentum and weight decay. Arashikage (`Hex63`) hardens this for RL by replacing zero initialization ($\mathbf{W}_0 = \mathbf{0}$, which traps $\operatorname{SiLU}(0)=0$ at zero gradient) with learned orthogonal meta-priors $\mathbf{W}_{\text{init}}^{(1,2)}$ and unrolling 16-step chunks during PPO updates.
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Sits at **Stage 3**—*after* the Stage 2 Bipartite Encoder (so memory keys/values summarize the whole scene rather than raw coordinates) and *before* the Stage 4 `Coconut` reasoning loop (so multi-step tactical planning is conditioned on historical match memory).

### 2.5 `Coconut` Multi-Step Continuous Thought & Dynamic 3D Focus Anchors
- **1. How It Works**: Instead of passing the 16 scene tokens through a fixed stack of distinct layers once, `Coconut` recurrently loops the 16 latent tokens $\mathbf{Z}^{(k)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 16 \times 384}$ through a shared reasoning block $K_t \in \{4, 6\}$ times. At each iteration $k$, every token updates a 3D focus coordinate $\mathbf{p}_{\text{lat}, l}^{(k)} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ in the arena, recomputes pairwise distances to all 64 entities, and performs cross-attention.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: Multi-step spatial chains—such as reading a corner double-tap, computing whether an ally or opponent reaches a rebound first, and choosing a third-man rotation spot—require sequential composition where step $k$ looks at a different region of the pitch based on the conclusion of step $k-1$.
- **3. What It Contributes**: Enables variable-depth test-time compute ($K=6$ passes when GPU latency head-room allows in `Tier 0/1`, gracefully stepping down to $K=4$ in `Tier 2/3` under heavy load) while physically shifting attention focus across the 3D arena on each pass.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Draws from *Training Large Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space (Coconut, Hao et al., Meta FAIR, Dec 2024)* and *Universal Transformers / PonderNet (Dehghani et al., 2019; Banino et al., 2021)*. Arashikage (`Hex63`) grounds each continuous thought step in 3D Euclidean geometry by attaching a learnable spatial focus coordinate $\mathbf{p}_{\text{lat}, l}^{(k)}$ to each latent token (inspired by *DAB-DETR / SparseBEV* 3D query anchors).
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Sits at **Stage 4 (Core Trunk)**, taking memory-enriched scene tokens from `Titans` and producing the final reasoned representation $\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{(K)}$ consumed by the skill bottleneck, motor heads, value critic, and world model.

### 2.6 Static-Shape `Soft-MoE` with Aux-Loss-Free Bias Balancing
- **1. How It Works**: Inside the `Coconut` block, the feed-forward network pairs **1 Shared SwiGLU Expert** ($d_{\text{ffn}}=512$) with **8 Specialist SwiGLU Experts** ($d_{\text{exp}}=192$). Rather than routing tokens via discrete Top-K index gathering, all 8 specialists are evaluated in a single fixed-shape `torch.einsum` and combined via softmax gating weights biased by a non-gradient homeostatic vector $b_e^{\text{bal}}$.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: Rocket League combines qualitatively distinct control regimes (ground dribbling, wavedashes, ceiling shots, flip resets, shadow defense). A single dense FFN suffers from gradient interference across these regimes, whereas standard Top-K sparse MoE uses dynamic control flow (`torch.nonzero` / variable-size token buffers) that is incompatible with `torch.cuda.CUDAGraph` capture.
- **3. What It Contributes**: Expands specialist model capacity across 8 mechanical domains with **zero dynamic branching**, keeping `100%` static tensor shapes for sub-2ms CUDA Graph replay while preventing expert collapse via non-gradient bias updates.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Combines *From Sparse to Soft Mixtures of Experts (Soft-MoE, Puigcerver et al., Google Brain, 2023)*, the *Shared + Fine-Grained Routed Expert* topology from *DeepSeekMoE (Dai et al., 2024)*, and *Auxiliary-Loss-Free Load Balancing (Wang et al., 2024)*, which adjusts routing biases $b_e^{\text{bal}} \leftarrow b_e^{\text{bal}} + \gamma(\frac{1}{E} - \bar{f}_e)$ outside the gradient graph so load balancing never distorts the primary RL policy gradient.
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Embedded as the **FFN sub-layer inside Stage 4 (`Coconut`)**, so every iteration of continuous thought can draw on different mechanical specialists.

### 2.7 Finite Scalar Quantization (`FSQ`, `1,000`-Code Discrete Skill Bottleneck)
- **1. How It Works**: Projects the ego car's final latent vector $\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_{\text{self}}^{(K)}$ to $\tilde{\mathbf{z}} = \tanh(\mathbf{W}_{\text{skill}}\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_{\text{self}}^{(K)}) \in (-1, 1)^{16}$ and rounds each scalar onto 5 uniform levels $\{-1.0, -0.5, 0.0, +0.5, +1.0\}$ using a straight-through estimator $\mathbf{z}_{\text{skill}} = \tilde{\mathbf{z}} + \operatorname{sg}(\operatorname{round}(2\tilde{\mathbf{z}})/2 - \tilde{\mathbf{z}})$. Grouping dimensions into 8 cyclic 3D triplets yields $8 \times 5^3 = 1,000$ discrete skill codes.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: Traditional VQ-VAE codebooks suffer from codebook collapse and require auxiliary commitment losses, while Gumbel-Softmax bottlenecks inject stochastic noise during PPO evaluation passes (violating $r_t(\theta_{\text{old}}) \equiv 1.0$).
- **3. What It Contributes**: Creates a deterministic, zero-parameter discrete skill interface that stabilizes downstream motor generation and gives the PFSP matchmaking league an explicit `1,000`-bin behavioral signature (via Jensen-Shannon divergence) to measure and reward mechanical diversity.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Based on *Finite Scalar Quantization: VQ-VAE Made Simple (Mentzer et al., Google Research, ICLR 2024)*, which showed that bounding each dimension with $\tanh$ and rounding to $L$ fixed scalar levels achieves nearly 100% codebook utilization without learned codebook vectors or commitment losses.
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Sits at the **boundary between Stage 4 (`Coconut` tactical trunk) and Stage 5 (low-level `60 Hz` motor heads)**, translating continuous spatial reasoning into a discrete skill intent before subtick controls are generated.

### 2.8 `K_sub = 4` (`60 Hz`) Action Chunking, `16`-Way Binary Sequences & Double-Jump Guard
- **1. How It Works**: Each outer policy step (`15 Hz`, `8` physics ticks = `66.7 ms`) outputs a chunk of $K_{\text{sub}} = 4$ subtick actions executed every `2` physics ticks (`60 Hz` = `16.7 ms`). Stage 1 predicts each binary button's 4-subtick trajectory $(b_1, b_2, b_3, b_4) \in \{0, 1\}^4$ as a single `16`-way categorical distribution (`0..15`) in causal order: `Jump[16]` $\to$ `AerialDoubleJumpMode d_dj[2]` $\to$ `Boost[16]` $\to$ `Handbrake[16]`.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: Holding a single action for 8 physics ticks (`15 Hz`) makes frame-accurate mechanics impossible: a speedflip or fast double-jump requires releasing Jump (`0`) for 2 ticks to create a rising edge (`0 -> 1`) on the next subtick. Furthermore, in RocketSim (`Car.cpp:708-709`), pressing Jump in the air when $|\text{pitch}| + |\text{yaw}| + |\text{roll}| \ge 0.50$ triggers a directional flip (often a backflip) instead of a vertical double-jump.
- **3. What It Contributes**: Gives the agent `60 Hz` button timing at `15 Hz` trunk inference cost, masks out physically impossible airborne jump patterns before sampling, and uses the $d_{\text{dj}}$ flag to automatically clamp stick attitude to $\le 0.36 < 0.50$ on the exact double-jump subtick.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Action chunking was established in robotics by *ACT: Action Chunking with Transformers (Zhao et al., RSS 2023)* and *Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023)* to bridge slow high-capacity backbones with high-frequency motor loops. Modeling $2^4 = 16$ joint binary sequences extends autoregressive sequence modeling to enforce exact hardware state-machine constraints (rising/falling edges).
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Sits at **Stage 5A (First half of the Actor head)** so that the discrete button plan and double-jump guard flag ($m_{\text{dj}, k}$) are fixed *before* Stage 5B generates continuous stick angles.

### 2.9 `Flow-GRPO`: 4-Step Euler-Maruyama Conditional Flow Matching in $\mathbb{R}^{20}$
- **1. How It Works**: Conditioned on the motor context and Stage 1's chosen button sequences, Stage 2 generates the 20 continuous stick values (`4` subticks $\times$ `5` axes: `throttle, steer, pitch, yaw, roll`) by starting from Gaussian noise $\mathbf{u}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_{20})$ and integrating a learned velocity field $v_\theta(\mathbf{u}_\tau, \tau, \mathbf{c}_t)$ over 4 Euler-Maruyama steps ($\Delta\tau = 0.25$) with bounded bridge noise $\sigma_{\tau_m}$.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: Standard unimodal Gaussian policies ($\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_\theta)$) cannot represent multi-modal continuous distributions: if a car can beat a challenging defender by steering left (`-0.9`) or right (`+0.9`), a Gaussian policy averages the two modes to `0.0` (driving straight into the challenge).
- **3. What It Contributes**: Enables expressive multi-modal continuous control in 4 lightweight linear steps while providing exact, closed-form Gaussian transition log-probabilities at each step ($\rho_{t, m}(\theta) = \exp(\frac{1}{20}\Delta\ell_{t, m})$) for stable PPO/GRPO reinforcement learning.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Built on *Flow Matching for Generative Modeling (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023)* and *Rectified Flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023)*, which learn straight-line ODE transport maps between noise and data, combined with *Flow-GRPO / DPPO (2024–2025)*, which convert deterministic flow ODEs into discrete-time stochastic bridges so policy gradients can optimize intermediate denoising steps directly via importance sampling.
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Sits at **Stage 5B (Second half of the Actor head)**, conditioned on Stage 5A's binary button embeddings, and feeds directly into the `60 Hz` C++ `SubtickPhysicsExecutor` (and into `JEPA` during live candidate search).

### 2.10 Dual-Head `4×51 Symlog HL-Gauss` Critic, `JEPA` World Model & Lockstep `G=8` `GRPO`
- **1. How It Works**:
  - **Distributional Critic (`HL-Gauss`)**: Outputs 51 categorical bin logits over a fixed bijective $\operatorname{symlog}(G) = \operatorname{sign}(G)\ln(1+|G|) \in [-10, +10]$ grid across **4 discount horizons** ($\gamma \in \{0.95, 0.98, 0.995, 0.9995\}$) on two heads: $p_\psi^{\text{priv}}$ (with training-only opponent context) and $p_\psi^{\text{eval}}$ (deployable).
  - **`JEPA` World Model & Best-of-16 Search**: Predicts the next latent state $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t+1}$ and dual rewards $(\hat{r}_{\text{dense}}, \hat{r}_{\text{sparse}})$ for 16 candidate action chunks in parallel, picking the highest $\text{CVaR}_\alpha$ Bellman score.
  - **Lockstep `G=8` `GRPO`**: Clones 102 mechanical drill scenarios across $G=8$ identical Bullet3 arenas (`816` arenas total) and normalizes returns within each 8-clone group: $A_{g,t}^{\text{GRPO}} = (G_{g,t} - \mu_G)/(\sigma_G + 10^{-4})$.
- **2. Why It Is Used**: Scalar MSE value regression suffers from scale sensitivity and averages together high-risk bimodal outcomes. Meanwhile, discovering multi-second aerial mechanics in full matches is bottlenecked by credit assignment noise, and sampling a single stochastic action in live play can occasionally pick a low-probability mistake.
- **3. What It Contributes**: `Symlog HL-Gauss` covers raw returns in $[-22025.5, +22025.5]$ without PopArt weight surgery; `G=8` `GRPO` isolates mechanical execution variance on drills; and live `JEPA` Best-of-16 search filters out bad stochastic samples before they reach the physics engine.
- **4. Science & Research Origins**: Integrates *DreamerV3 Symlog Transformations (Hafner et al., 2023)*, *Stop Regressing: Training Value Functions via Classification (HL-Gauss, Imani & White 2018; Farebrother et al., ICML 2024)*, *Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA, Assran & LeCun, CVPR 2023)*, and *Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO, Shao et al., DeepSeekMath / DeepSeek-R1, 2024–2025)*.
- **5. Placement in the Dataflow**: Sits at **Stage 6 (Evaluation, Search & Optimization)**—receiving $\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{(K)}$ from `Coconut` and candidate action chunks from `Flow-GRPO` to close both the **live `5.5 ms` control loop** (via `JEPA` + $p_\psi^{\text{eval}}$) and the **training update loop** (via $p_\psi^{\text{priv}}$ + `GRPO` + `PPO`).
